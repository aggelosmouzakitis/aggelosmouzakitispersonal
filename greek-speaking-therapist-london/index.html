Greek-speaking therapy for tech professionals in London

You built a serious career in one of the most demanding tech markets in the world, in a language and a culture that isn’t your first. This is therapy that speaks both — the work and the Greek part — so you don’t have to translate either one.

Who this is for

Greek-speaking founders, engineers, product managers, designers, consultants and executives who moved to London for the work and stayed for the career. Fintech, scale-ups, big tech, agencies, the consulting firms, the ones who ended up somewhere near the City whether they meant to or not.

People who are doing well on paper and privately carrying something the performance review doesn’t capture.

What London does to you

London rewards a very particular setting: always on, always slightly ahead of yourself, always available. It works, for a while. Then the off switch stops responding and you notice you haven’t had a genuinely undefended thought in months.

For a lot of Greek professionals here there’s a second layer underneath it: you didn’t just take a job, you left. There was a version of your life back in Greece, and family who measure success differently, and a quiet arithmetic running in the background about whether all of this was worth it. That doesn’t show up in standups. It shows up at 2am.

Why the Greek part matters here

You can do therapy in English. Plenty of people do. But there’s a difference between being understood and being fluent, and the things that actually run you — the family expectations, the guilt, the humour, the particular weight of being the one who left — tend to live in Greek.

Working in your own language means you don’t spend half the session explaining what a Greek mother’s phone call means, or why “just set a boundary” lands differently when family loyalty was never optional. We skip the cultural footnotes and get to the thing.

Why I also understand the industry

Before I trained as a psychotherapist I spent 18+ years in tech, in product and growth, building companies and advising more than 500 of them. So you don’t have to explain what a runway is, why a reorg is quietly terrifying, or what it costs to be the senior person everyone assumes has it handled.

I understand both sides at once — the London tech pressure and the Greek context underneath it — which is the whole point of coming to someone like me rather than one or the other.

What people bring

Work that never switches off→you’re present with nobody, including yourself
Success that doesn’t feel like it→the title went up, the relief never arrived
The senior-person isolation→no one to say the unpolished thing to
Family in Greece, life in London→guilt and distance you can’t fully resolve either way
Identity fused to output→a slow week feels like a threat to who you are

Not everyone brings all of it. Most people bring one, and find the others were connected.

How the work runs

It runs on two tracks at once. We work the inner pattern that’s actually driving the problem, and the real decision in front of you — the move at work, the thing you’re avoiding, the direction you’re unsure of. One without the other doesn’t hold.

More detail on how I work, and on the two audiences I work with most: founders and solopreneurs.

Sessions are online

I don’t have a room in London. I’m based in Ireland and work online, one to one, with Greek speakers wherever they are. In practice London is easy — we’re in the same time zone, so an early morning or an evening slot after work is straightforward.

Confidential, and I mean it

London tech is a small world and word travels. What you say stays between us. I don’t report back to anyone, and I don’t use identifiable client stories anywhere. If you want the detail before you commit, read exactly how I handle confidentiality.

Not a career coach

This isn’t CV strategy or a promotion playbook, and it isn’t management consulting. Those work on the outside of the problem. When the same pattern keeps producing the same result no matter how good your plan is, the plan was never the issue. That’s the part I work on — with a licensed psychotherapist’s training and an operator’s read on your actual situation.

Common questions

Do sessions happen in Greek or English?

Whichever you want, and you can switch mid-sentence — most people do. The point of working with a Greek-speaking therapist is that you never have to pick the language that loses the meaning.

Are you based in London?

No. I’m based in Ireland and work online. London clients are in the same time zone, so scheduling is simple. There’s no in-person option.

Is this therapy or is it business advice?

Both, deliberately. I’m a licensed psychotherapist and I spent 18+ years in tech. We work the personal pattern and the work problem in the same room, because for most of my clients they turn out to be the same problem.

I’m functioning fine. Is it “bad enough” to come?

Most of the people I see are functioning fine and that’s exactly the issue — the cost is invisible from the outside and very much not from the inside. You don’t need a crisis to have a reason.
