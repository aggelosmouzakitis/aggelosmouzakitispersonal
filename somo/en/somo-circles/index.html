Somo Circles · Therapeutic experience

Somo Circles

A small group therapy experience that combines controlled somatic shaking with facilitated conversation.

No previous experience with body-based practices or groups is needed.

View upcoming Somo Circles→See what happens in a session →

What it is

Two things in the same session

In Somo Circles you do simple body-based exercises that let a controlled shaking arise in the body. This is called somatic shaking.

The physical practice is combined with facilitated group therapy. There you can notice what you observed and connect the bodily experience with thoughts, feelings, tension or situations in your life.

Somatic shaking

What it is in practice

Somatic shaking is a natural trembling that can appear when the body tires or relaxes after some effort. It is not mysterious and it does not take away your control.

During the exercise you stay alert and you decide how far to go.

How it unfolds

  • You start from simple positions or movements.
  • These positions may create muscular fatigue and let the shaking appear.
  • You stay alert and in control throughout.
  • You can adjust the intensity up or down.
  • You can pause or stop whenever you want.
  • There is no need to prove anything or force a particular reaction.

What can be talked about

  • What you noticed in your body.
  • Emotions or memories that appeared.
  • Patterns of tension that repeat.
  • Your boundaries in the group.
  • How it feels to be seen by other people.
  • What the experience connects to in your current life.

The group therapy

The conversation in the group

The group conversation can happen before or after the physical practice, depending on the format of the session. The facilitator holds the frame.

Speaking is always an invitation and never a requirement. You share as much and whenever you want.

A session

What happens in a Somo Circle

A typical session moves through five stages.

01
Arrival and orientation
The facilitator explains the structure, your options and how you can stop at any point.
02
Gentle physical preparation
Simple movements that prepare the body, without demanding exercise.
03
Somatic shaking
The positions that let the shaking appear, at your own pace and intensity.
04
Facilitated group therapy
Space to notice and, if you want, to share your experience with the group.
05
Closing and integration
Settling, returning to your usual rhythm and time to take in the session.

The order can change depending on the format of the session and the group.

Who it may suit

It may interest you if you

Somo Circles can be useful for different people. A few examples:

  • Carry persistent tension in your body.
  • Spend a lot of time thinking and analysing.
  • Feel disconnected from bodily sensations.
  • Find it difficult to slow down.
  • Want to approach your emotions with more than conversation.
  • Are interested in the experience of a therapeutic group.

What it is not

To avoid any confusion

  • It is not a fitness class.
  • It is not a performance.
  • It does not promise a particular emotional release.
  • It does not replace medical care.
  • It is not individual psychotherapy.
  • It does not suit every person at every moment.

Control and boundaries

You decide how far to go

Control stays with you throughout. The facilitator gives clear instructions for how you can stop or rest.

See Safety and FAQ→

In practice you can

  • Adapt the exercises.
  • Lower the intensity of the shaking.
  • Change position.
  • Rest.
  • Leave an exercise.
  • Stay silent, if you prefer.

How to explain it to someone else

Somo Circles is a small group therapy session that combines controlled somatic shaking with facilitated conversation. Participants use simple body-based exercises, notice what comes up, and can explore the experience with the group if they want to.

For therapists and coaches

If you are referring someone

Somo Circles may suit people who want to connect verbal reflection with body awareness, inside a facilitated group.

The sessions are a group, experiential setting and do not replace the individual work someone may already be doing.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about Somo Circles

Do I need previous experience?

No. The exercises are simple and the facilitator explains everything from the start.

Do I have to talk?

No. Speaking is an invitation. You can take part in the physical practice and stay silent in the conversation.

Can I stop the shaking?

Yes. You can lower the intensity, change position, rest or stop completely whenever you want.

Is this the same as yoga?

No. There are simple body-based exercises, but the purpose is different. Somo Circles combines somatic shaking with group therapy.

Is Somo Circles psychotherapy?

Somo Circles includes facilitated group therapy. It does not offer the same level of individual assessment and support as individual psychotherapy.

Is this suitable alongside individual therapy?

For many people, yes. If you are already in individual therapy, it is a good idea to discuss it with your therapist.

What should I wear?

Comfortable clothes you can move in. Nothing special is needed.

How large is the group?

Groups are small, so there is room for each person. The exact number is shown on each event on Luma.

Where do sessions take place?

In person. The venue and area are listed on each event on Luma.

What should I do if I have a health concern?

If you have a health condition, injury, are pregnant or have had recent surgery, consult an appropriate health professional first. See the Safety and FAQ page as well.
