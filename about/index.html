I help tech high performers find genuine fulfilment without losing their ambition — breaking the deeper patterns behind burnout, shame-driven achievement, overthinking, and emotional fatigue, through high-trust advisory, body-based nervous system regulation (including somatic shaking techniques), and biomarkers.

Background

Before training as a therapist, I spent 18+ years in B2B SaaS growth, from early-stage startups through to IBM's enterprise portfolio, advising 50+ companies. I still work as a fractional growth advisor. That history means I understand the environment my clients operate in — founder stress, leadership pressure, career decisions, difficult conversations — without needing half a session to set the context.

How I work

High-trust advisory. Grounded in your real professional context rather than abstract coaching.

Nervous system regulation. The work includes the body, not just thoughts. I use somatic techniques — including neurogenic tremor work, which engages the body's natural stress-discharge mechanism to release tension held in the muscles after sustained pressure. This addresses what analysis can't: the chronic activation that keeps you wired after the laptop closes.

Biomarkers. HRV (heart rate variability) is the clearest objective signal of nervous system health. A sustained drop precedes burnout symptoms by weeks; strong overnight HRV signals genuine recovery, not just absence of work. If you wear an Oura, WHOOP, or Apple Watch, we use it. This turns abstract concepts like "nervous system dysregulation" into something trackable and visible.

Who I work with

Founders, executives, product and growth leaders, and senior operators in tech — people doing well by every external measure who sense their relationship with work, performance, and self-worth has become too costly.

The common thread isn't a specific role or company stage. It's that the internal cost of performing — the energy it takes to manage how you appear, absorb pressure, stay ahead, stay liked — has started to exceed what performance gives back. That's the point at which optimisation alone stops working, and a different kind of attention is needed.

The methodology

Most high performers I work with have already tried the cognitive approaches — reframing, journaling, productivity systems. They can describe their patterns clearly. The knowledge is there. What hasn't shifted is the felt sense underneath it.

That's because a lot of what drives high-performance burnout, shame-driven achievement, and chronic overthinking isn't primarily a thinking problem. It's stored in the body: the tight chest before a difficult conversation, the inability to switch off after work ends, the sleep that doesn't restore. Talk therapy addresses the meaning layer. It doesn't always touch what's happening neurologically.

The work I do integrates three things that rarely appear together:

High-trust advisory. I understand the environment my clients operate in — founder stress, leadership dynamics, career decisions at senior level — without needing it explained. That shared context is what makes the advisory feel accurate rather than generic.

Body-based nervous system regulation. I use somatic techniques, including neurogenic tremor work — a practice where the body's natural tremoring response is engaged to discharge stress held in the muscles and connective tissue. This isn't relaxation. It's the nervous system completing a stress cycle that was interrupted by chronic high performance. Most clients find it strange for the first few minutes, then surprisingly effective. It works in a register that analysis can't reach.

Biomarkers. I use HRV (heart rate variability) — measured by wearables like Oura, WHOOP, or Apple Watch — as an objective nervous system health signal. HRV measures how flexibly your autonomic nervous system responds between heartbeats. A sustained decline precedes burnout symptoms by weeks; a strong overnight HRV signals genuine recovery. For data-oriented people, this turns abstract concepts like "nervous system dysregulation" into something trackable, and makes the improvement visible rather than subjective.

Together, these three threads address what high performance costs at the level of mind, body, and measurable physiology — rather than just the story you tell about it.
