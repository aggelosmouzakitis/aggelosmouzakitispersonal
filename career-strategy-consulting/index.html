Common questions

Do I need to arrive with a business idea?

No. You can arrive with no idea, one vague direction or several competing ones. We start with the experience and evidence you already have, then decide what deserves testing.

Is the aim to quit my job?

No. The aim is to create options. You keep your job while you test whether people will pay for the work. If you later choose to leave, you do it with evidence rather than hope. A smaller independent income stream may also be enough.

What kinds of work can we build?

Usually consulting, fractional leadership, coaching, freelancing or a defined service. The label matters less than the buyer, the problem and a delivery model that fits your experience.

What if I already have one or two clients?

That can still be a fit. A few introductions prove that somebody may buy. They do not automatically give you a clear offer or a repeatable way to find the next client.

Is this career coaching?

Not in the conventional CV, interview and job-search sense. We are building independent paid work. Career questions may become part of it because the offer changes your options and can challenge how you see yourself professionally.

Is this business coaching or therapy?

Neither cleanly. I will have an opinion on the offer, pricing and route to market. If fear, approval or identity is shaping the execution, we can work there too. The business remains part of the work.

What if I know what to do but keep postponing it?

Then more information is probably not the answer. We work out whether the hesitation is useful caution, fear of exposure, a need to make the idea risk-free or evidence that you do not actually want this version of it. Those require different moves.

How much time will this take alongside my job?

We set a realistic pace around your current role. But there is no honest version that requires no execution. If you cannot protect consistent time for conversations, testing and follow-through, this will remain an idea.

Will you find clients for me?

No. I help you identify the buyer, package the offer, build the route to market and improve it using evidence. You still have to have the conversations, make the ask and deliver the work.

Is the audit just a sales call?

No. It has a standalone outcome. You leave with a direction to test, the assumptions that need evidence and a concrete next move, even if we never work together. If continuing makes sense, we discuss it after the work is done.
